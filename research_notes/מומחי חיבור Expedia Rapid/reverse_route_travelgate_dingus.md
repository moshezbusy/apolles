# Reaching Expedia Group hotel inventory "in reverse" through an intermediary (Travelgate, Dingus, hubs, wholesalers, host agencies)

> Research date: 26 Nov 2026. **Method note (read first).** Direct fetching is blocked in this session for every vendor and Expedia host tried (docs.travelgate.com, travelgate.com, dingus.es, zentrumhub.com, usa.expediataap.com, hostagencyreviews.com all fail to resolve). Pages were instead read through a page-content parser. Each finding below is tagged **[read]** (the full page text was retrieved and the quote was seen in it) or **[snippet]** (search-engine extract only; re-open before quoting to the client). Vendor marketing is tagged **(vendor claim)**. This file builds on `indirect_routes.md` in the same folder and does not repeat it.
>
> Three labels are used throughout and kept separate: **RESELLER** (sells Expedia inventory to a client under the intermediary's own Expedia contract), **CONNECTOR** (technical pipe only; client needs its own Expedia contract and credentials), **HOTEL-SIDE** (tool that pushes a hotel's rates out to Expedia as a sales channel; wrong direction).

## 1. Travelgate: how a Buyer gets connected to a Seller, who approves an Expedia connection, and what it costs

### Takeaway
Travelgate is a **CONNECTOR**. Its own documentation says the Buyer must first sign a commercial agreement with the Seller and then type the Seller-issued credentials into Travelgate's activation form, so for Expedia the approval and the API key still come from Expedia; Travelgate does not approve, sponsor or lend Expedia access. No "credentials included" or reseller model for Expedia was found in Travelgate's documentation, and no public price list could be read (set-up fee per supplier, booking fees, and unverified monthly tiers of EUR 450 and EUR 875 appear in sources).

### Cited Findings

#### The connection process, step by step
- "To obtain an access code from a Seller, you must establish a commercial agreement with the desired Partner and request activation via the Auto-Activations Form." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, HotelX Credentials](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/connectivity-products/for-buyers/hotel-x/hotel-x-credentials/)
- "All access details must be provided and confirmed by each Seller." Supplier authorization "may rely on a username and password or a Supplier-generated API key (not to be confused with the Travelgate API Key)". **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, HotelX Credentials](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/connectivity-products/for-buyers/hotel-x/hotel-x-credentials/)
- Two different keys exist. "The Travelgate API Key authenticates you to Travelgate… The Supplier API Key (when required) authenticates your connection to a specific Supplier. It is stored securely inside the access configuration in Travelgate." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, HotelX Credentials](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/connectivity-products/for-buyers/hotel-x/hotel-x-credentials/)
- Request flow: log in, "Connections" > "My Connections" > "Create a New Connection"; Step 1 select the Partner; Step 2 "complete the form using the credentials and connection parameters provided by the Seller"; Step 3 notifications; Step 4 review and confirm. **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- "Having a commercial contract with Travelgate is essential to request a new connection." "Both Buyers and Sellers can request a connection." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- "Make sure your credentials are active and fully functional before proceeding, as using inactive credentials can cause delays." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- Activation is a technical test, not a commercial approval: "During activation, a testing phase ensures the new credentials work properly. This includes a Booking Flow (Search and Quote queries) and Static Content downloads." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- "All accesses are delivered inactive, and it is the Partners' responsibility to activate them (both Buyers and Sellers should be aligned to carry out the activation)." **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- A common error is "requesting an activation with credentials not yet activated by the Seller", which breaks the automatic workflow. **[snippet]** — [Travelgate knowledge base, Important points when requesting a New Connection](https://knowledge.travelgate.com/before-activation-request) (URL now redirects to the docs home page; text seen only in search extract)
- Older Travelgate FAQ: "Do I need a commercial agreement with each of the suppliers I want to connect to? Yes, before connecting with any of the suppliers integrated on our platform, you should have a commercial agreement with them." **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Top 10 questions](https://blog.travelgate.com/the-top-10-questions-businesses-ask-themselves-before-joining-travelgatex)
- Same FAQ: Hotel-X "will allow you to easily connect to the suppliers you have a contract with", and Travelgate is "purely a connectivity platform" with no back-office services. **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Top 10 questions](https://blog.travelgate.com/the-top-10-questions-businesses-ask-themselves-before-joining-travelgatex)

#### Travelgate and Expedia specifically
- Travelgate was named an Elite Partner in Expedia Group B2B's Certified Technology Partners Program; Travelgate describes Elite as the tier "reserved for the partners it trusts to support its most demanding, highest-volume connections" and says the status "is reviewed annually". **[read]** (vendor claim about its own status; consistent with Expedia's directory noted in `indirect_routes.md`) — [Travelgate blog, Elite Partner of Expedia Group](https://blog.travelgate.com/travelgate-is-now-elite-partner-of-expedia-group)
- The announcement describes the benefit as "a connection to Expedia Group that is reliable, well-integrated, and built to scale" for "the Buyers and Sellers who rely on it". It says nothing about Travelgate arranging, sponsoring or approving a Buyer's commercial access to Expedia. **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Elite Partner of Expedia Group](https://blog.travelgate.com/travelgate-is-now-elite-partner-of-expedia-group)
- The spokesperson quoted is Travelgate's "Head of Supplier Relations" (Javier Lozano). **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Elite Partner of Expedia Group](https://blog.travelgate.com/travelgate-is-now-elite-partner-of-expedia-group)
- Expedia's own position on technology partners: the programme "is built for partners without the technical resources to connect Rapid API or who prefer to outsource integration", i.e. it presupposes the client is already an Expedia partner. **[snippet, carried over from `indirect_routes.md`]** — [Expedia Certified Technology Partners Program](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/solutions/build-your-travel-experience/rapid-api/certified-technology-partners-program)

#### Fees
- Two seller types. "Connecting to a Premium Partner is completely free as they assume the connectivity costs. Once you have certified your development, the Premium Partner will request the activation of the connectivity with you." "Connecting with a Standard Partner: you will be covering the costs for this connectivity, so once you have signed a commercial agreement with us, you can request the activation of the connectivity to any supplier in the Network." **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Top 10 questions](https://blog.travelgate.com/the-top-10-questions-businesses-ask-themselves-before-joining-travelgatex)
- "Do I have to pay for each supplier activation? Yes, there is a set-up fee for each supplier connected (except Premium Partners)." No amount given. **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Top 10 questions](https://blog.travelgate.com/the-top-10-questions-businesses-ask-themselves-before-joining-travelgatex)
- The activation form shows one of three payment types per connection: "FREE: No cost to you. Your Partner will cover the connectivity fee. STD: Standard booking fees apply for both you and your Partner. SUP: Both standard fees and supplements apply to you, but it's free for your Partner." This confirms Travelgate charges per booking on non-free connections; no rate is published. **[read]** — [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)
- Search extracts of Travelgate's "Service Packages" page show a Professional tier at "EUR 450/month" (or EUR 10M–50M GMV) and an Enterprise tier at "EUR 875/month" (or EUR 100M+ GMV). The live page returned "This page is gone!" when opened on 26 Nov 2026, so these numbers are **unverified** and may be withdrawn or seller-side pricing. **[snippet]** — [Travelgate service packages](https://www.travelgate.com/service-packages)
- No certification cost on the seller side ("there are no costs for certification"); Travelgate must certify a Buyer's HotelX development before live bookings. **[read]** — [Travelgate blog, Top 10 questions](https://blog.travelgate.com/the-top-10-questions-businesses-ask-themselves-before-joining-travelgatex); [Travelgate docs, HotelX Credentials](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/connectivity-products/for-buyers/hotel-x/hotel-x-credentials/)

### Inferences
- Who approves an Expedia connection requested through Travelgate: **Expedia**, in the sense that the form cannot be completed without Expedia-issued Rapid credentials, and Travelgate's test only checks that those credentials work. A Travelgate request therefore does not open a second approval path at Expedia; it is a step that happens after Expedia has already said yes.
- The only way Travelgate could help a declined applicant is informally (an introduction by its supplier-relations team to Expedia's account managers). Nothing in Travelgate's or Expedia's published material describes such a referral, so it should be asked as a direct question and not assumed.
- For this client Travelgate adds cost without solving the blocker: the agency already has a Rapid adapter, and Travelgate would require a Travelgate contract, HotelX development and certification, a possible set-up fee and per-booking fees, all on top of the missing Expedia contract.
- A real indirect use of Travelgate would be to buy from *other* Travelgate Sellers (bedbanks and wholesalers) that accept small buyers. That gives non-Expedia supply, each still under its own contract.

### Gaps
- Whether Expedia is a "Premium Partner" (free for the Buyer) or a Standard Seller in Travelgate could not be confirmed. A 2019 Premium Partner list seen in a search extract did not include Expedia ([Travelgate blog, 2019 summer Premium Partners](https://blog.travelgate.com/meet-some-of-our-2019-summer-premium-partners), snippet only). The current seller list sits behind login (app.travelgate.com/network).
- No Travelgate Seller was found that publicly states it resells Expedia inventory under its own contract. Travelgate's seller list is not public, so this is unverified, not disproven.
- No statement from Travelgate or Expedia on whether a Travelgate-originated request reaches a different Expedia team. No evidence either way.
- Actual Travelgate prices for a small buyer (set-up fee amount, per-booking fee, any minimum monthly fee) are not published in any page that could be read; must be requested from Travelgate sales.

## 2. Dingus: what it is, and whether an agency can get Expedia inventory through it

### Takeaway
Dingus is **HOTEL-SIDE**: a Palma de Mallorca channel manager and CRS that hotels buy to push their own rates and availability out to sales channels (OTAs such as Expedia and Booking.com, tour operators, bedbanks). The data flows hotel → Dingus → Expedia, which is the opposite of what the owner needs; nothing found shows Dingus offering any route for a travel agency to receive Expedia Group inventory.

### Cited Findings
- Dingus's own homepage headline: "Dingus, intelligent solutions that empower your hotel." "+1,500 hotels in 48 countries already optimise their distribution with Dingus." The call to action is a demo "with a hotel specialist". **[read]** — [Dingus homepage (EN)](https://www.dingus.es/en/)
- Customer testimonial on the same page (Lopesan Hotel Group): "Dingus is the right Channel Manager to manage, quickly, easily and effectively, the huge flow of data that allows us to reach the entire distribution channel". A second testimonial (Viva Resorts) describes optimising "room distribution across multiple channels". Both customers are hotel groups. **[read]** — [Dingus homepage (EN)](https://www.dingus.es/en/)
- Independent description: "Dingus is a leading provider of hotel distribution and connectivity solutions, offering advanced tools to optimize channel management, reservations, and revenue strategies… supports more than 1,500 hotels across 48 countries through a network of over 500 integrations." The Cloudbeds (hotel PMS) partnership of 29 April 2026 is framed as giving hotels "expanded access to key distribution channels". **[read]** — [Hospitality Net, Cloudbeds partners with Dingus](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4132139/cloudbeds-partners-with-dingus-to-unify-hotel-distribution-and-drive-revenue-growth)
- Products are listed on a hotel-software review site as "SmartChannel (DINGUS)", a channel manager, and "BookinCenter (DINGUS)", a central reservation system for hotel sales. **[snippet]** — [Hotel Tech Report, SmartChannel (DINGUS)](https://hoteltechreport.com/revenue-management/channel-managers/dingus-smartchannel)
- Dingus is noted for distributing hotel rooms and rates to major tour operators, which standard channel managers often do not do. **[snippet]** — [SIHOT, SIHOT and Dingus automate tour operator hotel reservation](https://sihot.com/en/?p=236245)
- Dingus has been a Booking.com connectivity partner since 2020. **[snippet]** — [Hosteltur](https://www.hosteltur.com/comunidad/nota/031807_el-consejo-asesor-de-conectividad-de-bookingcom-recibe-a-la-espanola-dingus.html)
- Marriott International approved Dingus as an eligible channel manager for its all-inclusive hotels (more than 30 properties). **[snippet]** — [Mallorca Diario](https://www.mallorcadiario.com/marriott-international-avala-el-channel-manager-de-la-mallorquina-dingus-para-sus-all-inclusive)
- How Expedia itself describes this category: channel managers "manage online distribution channels to sell accommodations inventory and manage bookings"; with connectivity software a *property* can "manage rooms, rates, and availability" on Expedia without logging in to Expedia Partner Central. This is Expedia's supply-side Connectivity Partner Program, separate from Rapid. **[read]** — [Expedia, Hotel connectivity providers](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/industries/hotels/hotel-connectivity-providers)
- Travelgate's documentation treats channel managers as a route by which a *Buyer* receives products it has contracted directly with hoteliers: the Inventory solution "lets you set up the products you've contracted with hoteliers and gives channel managers the tools to push availability and prices", and "activations of connections with Channel Managers via Travelgate should always be requested by the Buyers". **[read]** — [Travelgate docs home page, "Explore Travelgate APIs" (reached by redirect from this knowledge-base URL)](https://knowledge.travelgate.com/before-activation-request); [Travelgate docs, Quick Guide to Auto-Connections](https://docs.travelgate.com/kb/platform/app-features/connections/my-connections/guick-guide-to-auto-activations)

### Inferences
- The owner's premise about Dingus is reversed. Expedia is a *destination* for a Dingus hotel's rates, not a source. An agency cannot "join Dingus" to obtain Expedia rates.
- The only way a travel agency or tour operator meets Dingus is as a receiving channel for hotels it has **directly contracted**: the hotel loads the agency's contract rates in Dingus and Dingus pushes them to the agency's system (often via a hub such as Travelgate). That is a direct-contracting model with individual hotels, mostly Spanish and Latin American resort hotels, and has nothing to do with Expedia Group inventory.
- Dingus and Travelgate are both Palma de Mallorca companies and are often named together, which is probably why the owner grouped them; they sit on opposite sides of the trade (Dingus serves hotels, Travelgate connects buyers and sellers).

### Gaps
- Dingus's own product pages and channel list could not be opened beyond the homepage, so its Expedia connection is not confirmed from Dingus's site; it is inferred from its 500+ integrations and channel-manager role. Whether Dingus holds Elite or Preferred status in Expedia's Connectivity Partner Program was not checked (the list is in Expedia Partner Central).
- Whether Dingus is itself integrated in Travelgate's network was not confirmed from a source in this session.
- No Dingus statement was found, in either direction, about serving travel agencies as buyers; the conclusion rests on the consistent hotel-only positioning of every source read.

## 3. Expedia's "API downstream partners": what Expedia says, who they are, how a declined applicant gets referred

### Takeaway
Expedia's Rapid sign-up form contains a consent sentence confirming, in Expedia's own words, that there are "API downstream partners" who can give an unsuitable applicant access to "Expedia Group supply via their own connection to the Expedia Group B2B API". This is the first primary-source confirmation that a sanctioned no-own-contract route exists, but Expedia publishes no list of these partners and no procedure for requesting a referral; the wording describes Expedia passing the lead and the partner making contact by email.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim text on the form: "If my business is not suitable to connect directly with Expedia Group B2B, I agree that Expedia Group B2B may pass my details to one of its API downstream partners, who may then reach out to me by email about accessing Expedia Group supply via their own connection to the Expedia Group B2B API." **[read]** — [Expedia, Get started with Rapid API](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/join-us/rapid-api)
- Same form: "All applications will be reviewed case by case. We'll be in touch as soon as possible to discuss the best travel solutions for your needs." **[read]** — [Expedia, Get started with Rapid API](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/join-us/rapid-api)
- Expedia's B2B launch rules contain the matching mechanism on the partner side. Requirement GR3: "Downstream agents must agree to our Agent Terms and Conditions for accessing our inventory. Agents making a booking on behalf of a traveler must agree to our downstream agent agreement. A link to the downstream agent agreement should be provided for agents to review and accept prior to making a booking." **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, Lodging API launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Expedia case study of a partner acting in this role (technology platform): ZentrumHub's platform "helps their clients quickly launch travel booking apps that are pre-integrated with 100+ sources of supply, including Expedia Group inventory through their Rapid Lodging API integration"; "Rapid API's lodging inventory gave ZentrumHub and its clients competitive rates at over 800,000 properties"; ZentrumHub is "powering connections for travel companies worldwide who want to unlock Expedia Group inventory". **[read]** — [Expedia case study, How ZentrumHub became an Elite Technology Partner](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/how-zentrumhub-became-elite-technology-partner)
- Same case study: ZentrumHub reached "over $100 million in gross booking value in 2025", the partnership "only started in 2020", and it has a "dedicated account manager and account management team at Expedia Group". **[read]** — [Expedia case study, ZentrumHub](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/how-zentrumhub-became-elite-technology-partner)
- Expedia case study of a partner acting in this role (wholesaler): Overseas Travel Service (OTS), Japan, "serve a network of 5,000 travel agencies"; "As an Expedia Group partner for the past 12 years, OTS has integrated exclusively with Rapid Lodging API"; it offers agencies "competitive B2B distribution rates and discounted package rates"; "Rapid Lodging API is essential to OTS' B2B platform". **[read]** — [Expedia case study, OTS](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/ots-powers-booking-efficiency-with-rapid-lodging-api)
- A further Expedia case study describes Omnia Travel using Distribution and Business rates for agents through Rapid. **[snippet]** — [Expedia case study, Omnia Travel](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/case-studies/omnia-travel-boosts-distribution-rate)
- Expedia Group B2B describes its partner base as "over 250,000 global partners, combining travel advisors, technology resellers, and embedded travel programmes". **[read]** — [WiT, Expedia Group B2B: the invisible engine](https://www.webintravel.com/expedia-group-b2b-the-invisible-engine-powering-the-worlds-travel-bookings/)
- ZentrumHub's own answer to the question (vendor claim): "Can I get Expedia inventory without becoming a direct partner? Yes. Connect through an Expedia Certified/Elite Technology Partner like ZentrumHub — you inherit a live, certified Expedia connection without joining the vetting queue." **[read]** — [ZentrumHub, Expedia Hotel API](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/)

### Inferences
- "API downstream partner" is best read as: a company with its own approved Rapid B2B integration that Expedia allows to serve smaller agencies beneath it, with those agencies bound to Expedia by the click-through downstream agent agreement (GR3). The ZentrumHub and OTS case studies show the two forms this takes: a technology platform that exposes Expedia content to client apps, and a wholesaler that exposes it to agencies on a B2B platform.
- The consent sentence changes the status of the ZentrumHub claim from the earlier research pass. Expedia itself says such partners use "their own connection", and Expedia's case study says ZentrumHub's *clients* get Expedia rates. ZentrumHub is therefore a plausible, Expedia-recognised downstream partner. It is still not proven that ZentrumHub will onboard a client Expedia has declined, or on what commercial terms.
- Referral is Expedia-initiated according to the wording. A declined applicant who ticked the box may already have been passed on; if no partner has emailed, the practical move is to reply to the decline message and ask Expedia by name for a referral to an "API downstream partner" covering the agency's market, quoting the sentence above.
- Because the form sentence says the downstream partner contacts the applicant "by email", any unsolicited email from a company offering Expedia supply after the rejection should be checked against Expedia's public technology partner directory before engaging.

### Gaps
- Expedia publishes no list titled "API downstream partners". The names above (ZentrumHub, OTS, Omnia Travel) are inferred from Expedia case studies, not from an official downstream list. Whether the Certified Technology Partner directory and the downstream-partner set are the same group is unconfirmed.
- No Expedia page describes how to *request* a referral, how Expedia picks the partner, or whether referral happens automatically on decline.
- No first-hand account of an applicant being referred to a downstream partner was found (forums were not reachable).
- Which downstream partner, if any, serves agencies in Israel or the wider region is unknown.

## 4. Which wholesalers, bed banks, consolidators and B2B platforms are documented to resell Expedia Group inventory to sub-agents under their own contract?

### Takeaway
Hard evidence of an Expedia-sanctioned downstream model exists for only a few names, and only one of them (ZentrumHub) markets an API route that a foreign small agency could plug its own tool into; its contract structure remains unconfirmed. For every classic bedbank on the candidate list (Hotelbeds, WebBeds, TBO, RateHawk, Dida, Stuba, Bonotel, Travellanda, W2M, Smyrooms, PriceTravel) no source shows resale of general Expedia inventory, and the hotel-tech names (HotelRunner, DerbySoft, RateGain) are hotel-side connectivity, i.e. the wrong direction.

### Inferences
- The market splits cleanly. Bedbanks sell their own contracted rooms and compete with Expedia; none is shown reselling it. The companies Expedia itself showcases as serving downstream clients or agencies are a technology hub (ZentrumHub) and regional B2B wholesalers (OTS in Japan, Omnia Travel). So the realistic "reverse route" is a downstream partner of that kind, not a bedbank and not a channel manager.
- ZentrumHub is the only candidate where (a) the vendor says no Expedia onboarding is needed, (b) Expedia publicly confirms its clients receive Expedia rates, and (c) access is by API, which fits a custom-built tool. It is the lead worth a sales call. Three questions decide whether it is real for this client: who signs with Expedia (ZentrumHub alone, or the client too); who is merchant of record and who pays Expedia; and whether ZentrumHub will activate its pre-integrated Expedia connection for a client Expedia has declined directly.
- The client's existing Rapid adapter would not be reused on a downstream route: the tool would call the intermediary's unified API, not Rapid.
- Regional wholesalers such as OTS show the model exists, but they serve their home-market agencies through their own platform. No evidence suggests they onboard foreign agencies or expose Expedia content by API to a sub-agent's own software.

## 5. The Expedia TAAP "host agency" route: how it works, who offers it, and whether it gives API access

### Takeaway
The TAAP host route is real and simple: an agency already enrolled in TAAP gives an agent a registration (invite) link, and the agent's login is attached to that agency's account and created instantly, with no separate Expedia approval. It delivers the TAAP **website only** (no search or booking API), the only named network Expedia itself links to TAAP in sources found is TRAVELSAVERS, and the exact decline-message wording quoted in the brief could not be located publicly.

### Cited Findings
- Expedia support article, "How do I join an existing agency?": "The recommended way to register is by using a unique link provided specifically for your agency. You should contact your agency manager to request this link." After the form is submitted, "Your account will be created instantly, and you can begin using the Expedia TAAP platform right away." **[read]** — [Expedia Partner Solutions support](https://support.expediapartnersolutions.com/hc/en-us/articles/32488900036754-How-do-I-join-an-existing-agency)
- Same article, USA-only alternative: without a link, use the general TAAP registration form and "enter your agency's name exactly as it is registered in the Agency Trade Name field" so the account "is successfully associated with your agency". **[read]** — [Expedia Partner Solutions support](https://support.expediapartnersolutions.com/hc/en-us/articles/32488900036754-How-do-I-join-an-existing-agency)
- Expedia's US TAAP "how to register" page tells an agent joining under an agency to first confirm the host agency has a TAAP account and then ask it for an invite link to register as a sub-agent on its master account; an agency with no account registers itself and is reviewed in 24–48 hours. **[snippet]** — [Expedia TAAP USA, How to register](http://usa.expediataap.com/how-to-register)
- Manager side: the agency manager copies an invitation (with a link, or just the link) and shares it; the link "can be used again and again as you continue to add more agents to your account". **[snippet]** — [TAAP Academy, Managing agents](https://taapacademy.expediagroup.com/step-by-step-guide/travel-agency-managers/inviting-agents)
- Expedia runs dedicated US host-agency sign-up pages ("TAAP - Join TAAP - USA Host", "TAAP - Host agency promo - USA"). The pages now redirect to Expedia's general partner site, so their content could not be read. **[snippet, titles only]** — [Expedia, Join TAAP USA Host](https://expediapartnersolutions.com/jointaap-usa); [Expedia, Host agency promo USA](https://expediapartnersolutions.com/jointaap-host-agency-usa)
- Named network: "Independent agents in the TRAVELSAVERS network can also offer Expedia Group's extensive supply and competitive rates through TAAP." **[snippet]** — [Expedia newsroom, Expedia Group welcomes new partners](https://www.expedia.com/newsroom/expedia-group-welcomes-new-partners-to-its-global-travel-ecosystem/)
- Host Agency Reviews keeps an "Expedia Group" consortium page stating "the list below only shows host agencies within Expedia Group"; the list did not render when the page was read. A search extract of the same page showed Fora Travel listed. **[read for the page text; snippet for Fora]** — [Host Agency Reviews, Expedia Group](https://hostagencyreviews.com/consortia/expedia)
- Expedia's general positioning: "whether you're part of a retail agency or have recently joined a consortium", TAAP is available; registrations are reviewed within 48 hours. **[snippet]** — [Expedia, Travel agencies](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/industries/travel-agencies)
- International reach: TAAP "supports 12,000 travel agents and 400 agencies in Singapore alone". **[read]** — [WiT](https://www.webintravel.com/expedia-group-b2b-the-invisible-engine-powering-the-worlds-travel-bookings/). TAAP launched in the UAE in April 2025. **[snippet]** — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250423970229/en/Expedia-Group-Expands-Presence-in-the-Middle-East-Through-New-Travel-Partnerships)
- Third-party descriptions (agency blogs, not Expedia policy): "the host holds the credential; individual agents register their own accounts and link them to the agency"; registration accepts ARC, CLIA, IATA, TRUE or host agency credentials. **[snippet]** — [MainStreet Travel Agency](https://mainstreetagencytravel.com/news/how-to-become-an-expedia-travel-agent-taap-training-access-and-powerful-commission-secrets); [Mize](https://mize.tech/blog/expedia-taap-a-game-changer-for-travel-agents/)
- API: the TAAP back-office API launched June 2026 is post-booking only and "does not support searching or booking" (from `indirect_routes.md`). **[snippet, carried over]** — [TAAP Academy, back-office API](https://taapacademy.expediagroup.com/latest-news/product-updates/back-office-api)

### Inferences
- Joining through a host bypasses Expedia's review of the individual agency, because the account is created instantly under the host's already-approved profile. That makes it the fastest permitted way to sell Expedia rates after a decline.
- The price is that the joiner is a sub-user of someone else's account: bookings, tier and commission sit with the host, the host decides the commission split, and the agency has no TAAP relationship of its own.
- It does not help the booking tool. Agents would search and book on the TAAP website; at best the host (not the sub-user) could feed booking data out through the 2026 back-office API.
- The host model is built for individual advisors affiliated with a host agency, mostly in the US. A registered agency in another country would more naturally apply for its own TAAP account at the local point of sale; whether a US host would accept a foreign agency as an affiliate is a question for each host.
- Offering TAAP logins onward to the tool's external agent customers would collide with the TAAP agreement's ban on appointing sub-agents noted in the earlier research.

### Gaps
- The exact sentence in the brief (declined agencies told that "if there is a host agency they are approved to work with, they can request an invite link") was not found on any public Expedia page. The mechanism it describes is confirmed; the wording should be taken from the client's own decline email.
- Which host agencies and consortia hold a TAAP master account and will issue invite links: only TRAVELSAVERS (Expedia newsroom, snippet) and Fora Travel (directory listing, snippet) surfaced. Nothing was found for Nexion, KHM Travel, Outside Agents, Travel Leaders / Internova, or for hosts outside the US.
- No source states how commission is split between host and sub-agent, or whether the host can restrict products.
- Whether TAAP has an Israel point of sale, and whether a host arrangement is available there, remains unconfirmed.
- No first-hand agent accounts (Reddit, host-agency forums) could be retrieved.

## 6. Is any real route permitted by Expedia, and what are the practical downsides?

### Takeaway
Buying Expedia inventory through a downstream partner is permitted in principle: Expedia's own form advertises it and its B2B launch rules provide the legal hook (each downstream agent accepts Expedia's agent terms). The cost is structural: the agency has no contract, no account manager and no rates of its own with Expedia, it must rebuild against the intermediary's API, and it carries the intermediary's margin, credit terms and rule-compliance, with several Expedia display and payment rules flowing down to its tool.

### Cited Findings
- Sanctioned in Expedia's words: downstream partners give access "via their own connection to the Expedia Group B2B API". **[read]** — [Expedia, Get started with Rapid API](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/join-us/rapid-api)
- GR3: downstream agents must accept Expedia's downstream agent agreement before booking; localized agent terms are published. **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- GR1: Expedia's "names and logos may only be used on traveler-facing or agent-facing sites with our explicit written approval in advance as an exception." **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- GR2: a link to Expedia's terms and conditions must be shown in the site's terms or on the booking page. **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Pricing display: "The total price should match the API response, with no rounding or currency conversion permitted"; additional service or booking charges need a waiver from Expedia and must be shown separately as an "affiliate booking charge" or "affiliate service charge". **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Payor data (TR3): for Expedia Affiliate Collect partners Expedia "strongly recommends that you send the name of the actual end payor"; otherwise "the name of the company or legal entity that is paying us"; "required for regulatory/sanctions screening purposes". **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Support (ER1, agent-to-agent): "Your agents should have access to their original itinerary IDs should they require assistance from our customer support." **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Package rates: the agent must tick a box confirming the rate is sold "together with an approved transport component", and the package rate must be labelled and priced as one total. **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Member-only deals may be sold only "in a closed-user environment (for example, via login to a site, mobile app, or agent tool)". **[read]** — [Expedia Developer Hub, launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/reference/b2b-standalone)
- Expedia disclaims the intermediary: it "does not control, and is not responsible for, the acts or omissions of Technology Partners in the provision of their own products and services." **[snippet, carried over]** — [Expedia Certified Technology Partners Program](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/solutions/build-your-travel-experience/rapid-api/certified-technology-partners-program)
- ZentrumHub's integration guide says a certification review must pass before live supplier credentials are enabled (from `indirect_routes.md`), which contradicts "API certification: none on your side" on its sales page. **[snippet vs read]** — [ZentrumHub integration guide](https://docs-hotel.prod.zentrumhub.com/docs/getting-started-2); [ZentrumHub, Expedia Hotel API](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/)

### Inferences
- Route-by-route verdict for an agency with no Expedia contract:

| Route | Delivers bookable Expedia rates without own Expedia contract? | Who approves | Contracting party with Expedia | Cost known |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travelgate | **No** (connector) | Expedia must already have approved | The agency itself | Travelgate contract; set-up fee per supplier; per-booking fees; amounts unpublished |
| Dingus | **No** (hotel-side, wrong direction) | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Expedia-referred API downstream partner | **Yes in principle** (Expedia's own wording) | The downstream partner, under Expedia's rules | The downstream partner | Unknown |
| ZentrumHub pre-integrated connection | **Claimed yes** (vendor), partly corroborated by Expedia case study | ZentrumHub (claimed; plus its own certification review) | Not stated; presumably ZentrumHub | "Flat SaaS pricing", amount unpublished |
| Bedbanks (Hotelbeds, WebBeds, TBO, RateHawk, Dida and others) | **No evidence** of Expedia content | Each bedbank | n/a | n/a |
| TAAP via host agency invite link | **Yes, website only**; no API | The host agency (account created instantly) | The host agency | Free at Expedia; host's own fees and split |

- Practical downsides of any downstream route: (1) no contract, account manager or negotiated rates with Expedia, so no path to distribution-rate tiers; (2) the intermediary's margin or SaaS fee sits on top; (3) the intermediary is normally the party paying Expedia, so the agency takes its credit terms, deposit and insolvency risk; (4) support and disputes run through the intermediary using Expedia itinerary IDs; (5) access ends if the intermediary loses its Expedia status (Elite is "reviewed annually") or drops the client; (6) the existing Rapid adapter is not reused; (7) the tool still has to meet Expedia's display, terms-link, payor-data and agent-agreement rules, and cannot use the Expedia name without written approval; (8) the sub-agents using the tool are a further tier below the agency, and whether a downstream client may itself serve external sub-agents must be confirmed in writing.
- A downstream period could later support a direct re-application, since it produces real Expedia booking volume, but that volume is recorded under the intermediary's account, so the agency should keep its own booking statistics.
- Suggested order of action: ask Expedia (replying to the decline) for a named API downstream partner; in parallel put the three contract questions to ZentrumHub and, if wanted, to the other Elite technology partners; treat Travelgate as relevant only after a contract exists; drop Dingus.

### Gaps
- The Rapid partner agreement and the downstream agent agreement text were not retrieved, so the exact sub-distribution limits (for example whether a downstream client may expose the content to its own sub-agents, or re-expose it by API) are unknown.
- No source gives the commercial model a downstream partner applies to its clients (net rate plus mark-up, commission share, or flat fee) or who is merchant of record in that chain.
- No documented case was found of a directly declined applicant being onboarded through a downstream partner, nor of Expedia objecting to one.
- Whether going live through a downstream partner helps or harms a later direct application is not addressed in any source.
