# Does Expedia refuse or no longer onboard Israeli partners for Rapid API and for TAAP? (as of 26 Nov 2026)

> **Method and limits (read first).** Research done 26 Nov 2026. Direct fetching (WebFetch, curl) was blocked for every external host, so pages were read through a paid page-parsing tool and Google results were pulled through a SERP tool (English and Hebrew, Israel and US locations). About 50 calls. **Pages read in full** (not snippets): TAAP Academy support page, TAAP Academy FAQ, expediataap.com home page, Rapid "Locale details" page, IAS 2013 and 2017 articles, Passport News 2014 article, TravelPress and Travelweek 2022 articles, Breaking Travel News July 2026 article, one public LinkedIn post. **Snippet only** (not opened): Discover the World website lists, one LinkedIn profile, TheMarker 2011, Reuters / Al Jazeera 2025. Not reachable at all: Facebook groups, Reddit threads, the TAAP sign-up form itself, the TAAP agreement, LinkedIn profiles.
> Source-type labels used below: **[OFFICIAL]** Expedia's own pages (used for hard facts only), **[TRADE]** trade press, **[REP]** Expedia's sales representative's own website, **[FIRST-HAND]** an individual's own public post, **[SNIPPET]** seen only as a Google extract.
> No client details were used in any query.
> For three sources the tool returned only a Google redirect link; they are cited with the site's domain plus the exact title and date so they can be found, and are marked "(exact URL not captured)".
>
> **Headline:** independent sources confirming that Expedia stopped connecting Israel to Rapid = **0**. Independent sources confirming that TAAP refuses Israel = **0**. Official Expedia documentation that points the other way for Rapid = **1** (Israel is a listed supported point of sale, shekel is a listed supported currency). For TAAP the official list of points of sale has **no Israel entry**, which fits "no Israeli TAAP site" but does not by itself prove "Israeli agencies are refused".

## 1. Is Israel a supported country for Expedia TAAP sign-up in 2026? Was it ever, and when and why did that end?

### Takeaway
There is **no Israeli TAAP point of sale in 2026**: Expedia's own support directory lists 34 country sites plus a Europe catch-all, and Israel is not among them (high confidence, 1 official source read in full). Israel **was** actively served from July 2013 until at least February 2017 through the representative Discover the World Israel, apparently on the **US** point of sale; I found no announcement of that ending, and no source that says Israeli agencies are now refused.

### Cited Findings

The contact e-mail domains identify 34 dedicated points of sale: Argentina, Australia, Belgium, Brazil, Canada, China, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Philippines, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, United Arab Emirates, United Kingdom, United States, Vietnam. In the alphabetical block "G–M" the entries run Ireland (.ie) then Italy (.it); there is no Israel entry and no .co.il address. - **[OFFICIAL, same page]** The only catch-all is for Europe: "For European countries who do not have a dedicated point of sale listed above, reach out to the contacts below" (accountmanagement@expediataap.eu, +44 0203 564 5660). ### Inferences
- The 2014 Planeto statement (ranked among agencies "in the USA") plus the fact that Discover the World was TAAP's US/Canada representative strongly suggest Israeli agencies were never given their own point of sale: they were enrolled on the **US site (expediataap.com)** and looked after locally by Discover the World Israel. That is consistent with a 2026 Israeli application being answered by "Expedia TAAP US".
- The absence of Israel from the 2026 support directory is therefore not new evidence of an exclusion. Israel was not a dedicated point of sale even when the programme was being actively sold there.
- What changed, if anything, is the local representation: nothing after February 2017 shows a TAAP representative in Israel, and Expedia's 2025–26 regional build-out (UAE, Saudi Arabia) does not mention Israel. "Israel is no longer actively worked as a TAAP market" is a reasonable reading; "Israeli agencies are refused" is not shown by any source.

### Gaps
- No source states when or why the Discover the World Israel representation of TAAP ended, or whether it ended at all.
- The list of the 53 markets added in 2022 and the list behind "125 markets including hybrid POS" were not found, so I cannot say whether Israel is formally one of the hybrid markets.
- The country dropdown of the TAAP sign-up form could not be read (the form is behind a script the tool did not render). This is the single most decisive official fact still missing; a person can check it in two minutes on expediataap.com and expediataap.eu.

## 2. Do Israeli agencies sign up through another country's TAAP site, and what do the eligibility rules say about location, licences and host agencies? What does a "TAAP US / host agency invite link" reply mean?

### Takeaway
Historically yes: Israeli agencies appear to have been enrolled on the US site. The public TAAP pages I could read publish **no rule on agency location**, and the published credential requirements come only from third-party guides. A decline that points the applicant to a host agency's invite link is worded like a US accreditation decline, not like a country exclusion; I could not find a public copy of that template, so this remains an inference.

### Cited Findings
- **[TRADE, Israeli, 7 Dec 2014]** Planeto's Israeli agencies were counted within TAAP's US agency ranking (top 100 of 12,000 US agencies). — [Passport News](https://passportnews.co.il/) (exact URL not captured; title as in section 1)
- **[OFFICIAL, read in full]** The US TAAP home page invites agents to "Sign in to your existing account or register for a new account" and states no country restriction on the visible page. — [expediataap.com](https://www.expediataap.com/)
- **[OFFICIAL, read in full]** Commissions are paid monthly to "the Expedia TAAP-registered travel agency", never to individual advisors; the agency manager must supply banking details and a **tax form**, and the payment provider depends on the agency's point of sale. — [TAAP Academy FAQ](https://taapacademy.expediagroup.com/faq)
- **[Vendor guide, from an earlier pass, not Expedia]** TAAP "only accepts travel agencies with qualifying credentials: IATA, ARC, CLIA, or True" and has no minimum sales requirement. — [AltexSoft on Medium](https://altexsoft.medium.com/expedia-partner-solutions-for-travel-agencies-expedia-taap-templates-and-eps-rapid-api-bde62ea21608)
- **[OFFICIAL, from an earlier pass]** The TAAP agreement (EU version, effective 1 April 2025) forbids appointing sub-agents but lets an agency invite "Sub-Users" for whom it is responsible, which is the mechanism behind a host agency inviting its advisors. — [Expedia Travel Agency Affiliate Agreement, EU](https://www.expediataap.eu/)
- **[FIRST-HAND / social, SNIPPET]** US host agencies publicly advertise that "our agents now have access to Expedia TAAP", and Reddit has a thread titled "host agency: expedia taap only?" (36 comments, about two years old). This confirms that joining TAAP under a host agency's account is a normal US route. Threads not opened. — [r/travelagents](https://www.reddit.com/r/travelagents/)
- **[Search result, negative]** Two targeted searches for the wording of a TAAP decline that mentions a host agency invite link returned no public copy of such an e-mail.

### Inferences
- "Request an invite link through your host agency" is US-market vocabulary (host agencies are a US/Canada structure). It reads as the standard answer to an applicant whose own accreditation the US team could not validate (ARC, IATA/IATAN, CLIA or TRUE number), not as "we do not serve your country". If Israel were barred as a country, pointing the applicant to another way in would make little sense.
- Two practical obstacles are specific to a foreign agency on the US site and could explain a decline without any Israel policy: the US accreditation check, and the tax-form and payment-provider step, which is organised per point of sale.
- Whether a non-US agency is still allowed to hold its own account on the US site in 2026 is not stated anywhere I could read.

### Gaps
- Official TAAP eligibility text (accepted accreditation types, whether an Israeli IATA or TIDS number or an Israeli travel-agent licence is accepted, and on which site) was not retrievable.
- The US TAAP agreement's territory clause was not retrieved.
- No first-hand account from an Israeli agent about signing up for TAAP in 2024–2026, in Hebrew or English.

## 3. Is there evidence that Expedia Group B2B has paused or stopped signing new Rapid API partners based in Israel?

### Takeaway
No. I found **zero** statements, trade items, LinkedIn posts or forum reports, in English or Hebrew, that Expedia stopped or limited onboarding of Israeli Rapid partners in any period. Expedia's own current documentation lists Israel as a supported Rapid point of sale and the shekel as a supported currency, and an Israeli company is shown as a Rapid partner. The claim is unconfirmed and the available evidence leans against a formal country exclusion (medium confidence); an informal decision not to take on small Israeli partners can be neither shown nor ruled out from public sources.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL, © 2026, read in full]** Rapid "Supported points of sale" table includes "Israel | IL | ISR | Lodging: Yes" (Car: not supported, as for most countries). — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[OFFICIAL, same page]** Rapid "Supported currencies" table includes "Israeli New Shekel | ILS | Lodging: Yes". Only 38 currencies are listed, so ILS is a deliberate inclusion. — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[OFFICIAL, same page]** "Rapid does not support bookings, directly or indirectly, made on any of the following points of sale: North Korea." Countries such as Iran, Iraq, Syria, Cuba, Yemen and Afghanistan are simply absent from the supported table, while Israel is present. The page says the list "is continuously updated". — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[Conflict with an earlier pass]** An earlier research note, working from a search extract of a different Developer Hub page, recorded the excluded points of sale as "Cuba · Syria". The page read in full today names only North Korea. Either the pages differ or the list changed; in both versions Israel is not excluded. — [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs); [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[OFFICIAL, from an earlier pass, not re-read today]** Arbitrip appears among the partner logos on Expedia's Rapid API and travel-agency pages. — [Expedia Rapid API page](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/solutions/build-your-travel-experience/rapid-api)
- **[TRADE, 1 July 2026, read in full]** Arbitrip, "recently acquired by Talma Travel Solutions", launched a white-label hotel platform inside El Al's website for Matmid members, with "more than 1.5 million properties" and closed-user-group rates. The article does not name Expedia or any supplier. — [Breaking Travel News](https://www.breakingtravelnews.com/news/article/arbitrip-and-el-al-airlines-announce-first-of-its-kind-travel-booking-partn/)
- **[Search result, negative]** Hebrew searches (אקספדיה Rapid / B2B / EPS / API ישראל, אקספדיה שיתוף פעולה with the names of Issta, Gulliver, Eshet Tours, Kavei Hufsha, Daka 90, Ophir Tours) and English searches (Expedia Israel partners suspended / stopped / no longer, 2025–2026) returned nothing about Rapid onboarding in Israel. The Hebrew B2B query returned only global items and the Rapid locale page itself.
- **[From an earlier pass]** Zero independent first-hand reports, for any country, that the applicant's country changed a Rapid decision; vendors attribute selectivity to volume and business model. — [ZentrumHub, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/expedia-rapid-hotel-api-integration/); [StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)

### Inferences
- A company that had decided not to contract in Israel would have no reason to keep Israel and the shekel in a short, curated list of supported points of sale and currencies, or to display an Israeli-founded, Israeli-owned partner. This is circumstantial but it is the only hard evidence available, and it points one way.
- The supported-POS table is about where the traveller buys, not where the partner is registered. It shows Expedia sells into Israel through Rapid partners; it does not prove Expedia is signing new Israel-registered partners this year.
- A decline "with no reason" is the normal outcome for small applicants everywhere (see rejection_and_reapply.md), so the decline is not evidence of an Israel rule.
- The industry remark may be a distorted version of something narrower and true, for example "there is no Expedia B2B account manager for Israel any more", or "TAAP has no local representative now". That is a hypothesis, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No Expedia statement either way. No trade article. No first-hand Israeli account of a Rapid application.
- The Rapid partner agreement and the contracting Expedia entity for an Israeli partner are not public.
- Whether Arbitrip's Rapid contract is recent, and which legal entity holds it, is unknown, so Arbitrip cannot show that *new* Israeli partners are being signed in 2026.

## 6. Do Expedia's public point-of-sale, currency, tax or legal facts make Israeli partners harder to contract?

### Takeaway
Nothing in the public Rapid documentation disadvantages Israel: the Israeli point of sale and the shekel are both supported for lodging. The only Israel-relevant friction visible in public material is on the TAAP side (tax form and payment provider tied to the point of sale, and no Israeli point of sale).

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Israel (IL) supported point of sale for lodging; ILS supported currency for lodging; only North Korea named as unsupported. "Transactions must specify the country code that matches the point of sale being used by the traveler." — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Hebrew is not among the supported Rapid content languages (Arabic, Turkish, Greek and about 35 others are), so an Israeli partner receives content in English or another supported language. — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **[OFFICIAL]** TAAP: banking details and a tax form are mandatory before commission is paid; provider varies by point of sale. — [TAAP Academy FAQ](https://taapacademy.expediagroup.com/faq)

### Inferences
- There is no documented technical, currency or sanctions reason for Expedia to avoid Israeli Rapid partners. Any reluctance would have to be commercial (market size, account-management capacity, credit and payment risk assessment) and would not appear in public documents.

### Gaps

## 9. Verdicts, confidence, and what would settle each one

### Takeaway
Neither half of the "Expedia no longer connects Israel" claim is confirmed. For Rapid the evidence leans against it; for TAAP the truthful statement is narrower: there is no Israeli TAAP site or visible local representative, and Israeli agencies seem always to have been handled through the US site.

### Cited Findings
- **Verdict 1 — TAAP and Israel in 2026.** No dedicated Israeli point of sale: **confirmed, high confidence** (1 official source read in full). "TAAP refuses Israeli agencies": **neither confirmed nor refuted**; 0 sources say so, and the historical model (US site plus local representative) explains both the "TAAP US" reply and the absence from the list. — [TAAP Academy Support](https://taapacademy.expediagroup.com/support); [Passport News 2014](https://passportnews.co.il/)
- **Verdict 2 — Rapid and Israel.** "Expedia stopped signing Israel-based Rapid partners": **not confirmed; 0 supporting sources; evidence leans against a formal exclusion, medium confidence** (1 official documentation page listing Israel and ILS as supported, plus 1 official partner-logo listing of an Israeli company from an earlier pass). — [Rapid Locale details](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/resources/locale-details)
- **Verdict 3 — Israeli users.** Current: Arbitrip (Rapid; 1 official plus trade corroboration of nationality and ownership). Historical: Planeto (TAAP, 2014, 1 trade source), Discover the World Israel (TAAP representative 2013–2017, 2 articles from one trade site), Diesenhaus (2011, 1 trade snippet), IGT (about 2005). Contracting entities: unknown in every case. — sources in section 4
- **Verdict 4 — Practical route.** Not established for 2026 (0 current sources). Best-supported historical route: TAAP on the US site via a local representative or consortium. — sources in section 8

### Inferences
- The most economical explanation of everything observed: Expedia never built Israel as its own B2B market; TAAP access ran through a third-party representative on the US site; that local channel has faded; and Rapid applications from small agencies are declined on scale grounds as they are everywhere. An industry insider experiencing this would naturally summarise it as "Expedia doesn't connect Israel any more".

### Gaps
- What would settle it, in order of effort:
  1. **Look at the sign-up forms.** Open the registration form on expediataap.com and on expediataap.eu and check whether "Israel" is selectable as agency country. If it is on neither, Israel is not a self-service TAAP market.
  2. **One written question to TAAP** (accountmanagement@expediataap.com, copy accountmanagement@expediataap.eu and accountmanagement@expediataap.ae): "We are a licensed travel agency registered in Israel with [accreditation type and number]. Which Expedia TAAP point of sale accepts agencies registered in Israel? If none does, please confirm that Israel is not currently a supported TAAP market, and whether joining as a user under an existing TAAP agency account is permitted for an Israeli agency."
  3. **One written question to Expedia Group B2B about Rapid**: "Is Expedia Group B2B currently contracting new Rapid API partners whose legal entity is registered in Israel and whose point of sale is IL? If not, is that a market-level decision or specific to our application, and is access through one of your certified technology partners open to an Israel-registered agency?"
  4. **Ask the former channel**: Discover the World (info@discovertheworld.com, or its Expedia TAAP sales team) whether it still represents TAAP for Israel and, if not, since when and who does.
  5. **Ask peers**: Planeto's management whether its TAAP account is live and open to affiliated agencies; Arbitrip/Talma whether their Expedia connection is direct and whether they distribute to agencies.
