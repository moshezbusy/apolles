# Expedia Rapid API: why applications are rejected and what works on a second attempt

> **Provenance note for the report writer (read first).** Research date: 26 Nov 2026. In this session direct page fetching was blocked at network level (developers.expediagroup.com, partner.expediagroup.com and stayapi.com all failed DNS resolution), so **every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from a full read of the page**. Quoted wording is as returned by the search tool and should be treated as "probably verbatim, not verified in context". No client details were used in any query.
>
> **Headline on evidence quality:** independent first-hand evidence is **very thin**. ~40 searches across Reddit, Hacker News, Indie Hackers, Quora, Stack Overflow, Medium, dev.to and LinkedIn surfaced **zero** accounts of a Rapid-era applicant who was rejected and later approved, **zero** public copies of the "when an opening becomes available" email, and **zero** published thresholds. What exists: (a) official Expedia developer-hub text on the approval/launch process, (b) vendor blogs with a commercial interest in portraying direct access as hard, (c) three dated individual anecdotes (2010, 2016, 2021) from the EAN/EPS era.
>
> **Source-type labels used:** [OFFICIAL] = Expedia Group page or filing; [VENDOR] = marketing blog of a company selling integration/aggregation/data (treat as claim); [FIRST-HAND] = individual's own account; [PRESS] = trade or financial press.

## 1. What criteria does Expedia use for Rapid API partners (volume, years, live product, traffic, business model, licences, PCI, payment model, credit)?

### Takeaway
Expedia publishes **no** numeric eligibility criteria (no minimum volume, revenue, years in business, traffic, deposit or licence requirement was found in any official source); officially it only says you must "become a partner and be approved", then pass a launch-requirements site review. All statements about *how* applicants are judged (projected booking volume, business model, market, existing product, traffic) come from vendor blogs, the most specific of which is seven years old.

### Cited Findings
**Official process (what Expedia itself states)**
- [OFFICIAL, current] "Once you have become a partner and been approved to integrate with Rapid API, you will need to obtain your API key." The key stays in restricted development mode until site review and production approval; "when you're approved to go live, your business development manager will enable your API key for production use." — [Expedia Developer Hub, Rapid "Get started"](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started)
- [OFFICIAL, current] Launch requirements "set the standards your integration must meet before we permit your site, service, or app to go live"; "when you complete development on your integration, you must request and pass a site review by our support team." — [Rapid launch requirements overview](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/launch-requirements)
- [OFFICIAL, current] Launch requirements are split into those applying to any implementation and specific ones for B2B or B2C usage; separate "B2B standalone" and "B2C standalone" requirement pages exist. — [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs); [B2B standalone launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/b2b-standalone); [B2C standalone](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/b2c-standalone)
- [OFFICIAL, current] The official entry point is a "Get Started with Rapid API - Become a Partner" contact form. The form's actual fields could not be retrieved. — [partner.expediagroup.com join-us/rapid-api](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/join-us/rapid-api)

**PCI and payment model**
- [OFFICIAL, current] Property Collect "requires you to be PCI compliant because you handle credit card information." — [Rapid: Property collect payments](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/lodging/booking/property-collect)
- [OFFICIAL, current] An Attestation of Compliance (AOC) "must be supplied as evidence of PCI compliance when using property collect, or when Expedia Group is the merchant of record" (applicable partners only). — [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs)
- [VENDOR, AltexSoft, undated in results] "EPS Checkout" is described as a white-label card-collection interface that removes the need for the partner's own PCI compliance. — [AltexSoft on Medium](https://altexsoft.medium.com/expedia-partner-solutions-for-travel-agencies-expedia-taap-templates-and-eps-rapid-api-bde62ea21608)
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub, 2026 guide] Revenue is "commission-based (15–25% post-stay) under Expedia Collect, or net rate markup under Partner Collect". Single-source figure, unverified. — [ZentrumHub Expedia guide](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/expedia-rapid-hotel-api-integration/)

**Volume, business model, track record (all non-official)**
- [VENDOR, AltexSoft, c. 2019 — "about seven years old" per search metadata] "Although Expedia doesn't publicly disclose its enrollment terms", reviewers consider "revenue and growth potential, existing solution, website traffic, average number of bookings, and IATA and other compliance." This is the most specific list of criteria found anywhere, but it dates from the EPS era. — [AltexSoft: Expedia TAAP & Rapid API Partner Solutions](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/expedia-taap-rapid-api-partner-solutions/)
- [VENDOR, StayAPI (sells a hotel-data API), 2026] "Getting credentials requires a partner application with a business case and projected booking volume." The application "asks the commercial questions: what you're building, your business model, and your projected booking volume. Expedia Group reviews applications against revenue potential." Data-only/monitoring use cases do not qualify because Rapid monetises through bookings. "A funded travel startup with a booking product and volume projections has a real shot." Also says approval takes weeks to months and applications "tend to stall or go unanswered". — [StayAPI: Expedia Rapid API – Who Qualifies](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub (certified Expedia reseller/aggregator), 2026] Expedia "evaluates applicants on their business model and projected booking volume — and prioritises higher-volume partners"; it "vets applicants on booking volume, market, and coverage"; direct access means "passing their selective vetting — often several months, and smaller platforms are frequently declined." Estimates 2–4 weeks for the partner application and 12–20 weeks application-to-production. Says negotiated terms include "payment model, commission rates or net rate schedule, minimum volume commitments, and contract terms." — [ZentrumHub Expedia Hotel API page](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/); [ZentrumHub guide](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/expedia-rapid-hotel-api-integration/)
- [VENDOR/individual blog, dev.to, undated in results] Rates Expedia Rapid approval difficulty as high: "requires corporate entity and business track record." — [dev.to guide to hotel affiliate APIs](https://dev.to/kouta222/complete-guide-for-individual-developers-recommended-hotel-affiliate-apis-by-region-4c1e)
- [VENDOR, phptravels, 2026] You apply through Expedia Group partner solutions; after approval you get an API key and shared secret with sandbox access before the launch review; "Expedia does not charge a published fee for Rapid API access." — [phptravels: Expedia API Integration](https://phptravels.com/expedia-api-integration)
- [VENDOR, apihiver, 2026] Ranks Expedia Rapid #4 of 10 hotel APIs by ease of access, in an "apply, then certify" tier; no free tier; no approval timeline given. — [apihiver: Top Hotel APIs in 2026](https://apihiver.com/blog/top-hotel-apis)

**Licences (IATA / ARC / national licence)**
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub, 2026] "IATA accreditation is not required for Expedia Rapid API access. Any qualified travel business can apply to Expedia Partner Solutions regardless of IATA status." — [ZentrumHub guide](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/expedia-rapid-hotel-api-integration/)
- [VENDOR, AltexSoft, c. 2019] Lists "IATA and other compliance" among factors reviewers consider for EPS — i.e. a factor, not a stated hard requirement. Partially conflicts with ZentrumHub above. — [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/expedia-taap-rapid-api-partner-solutions/)
- [VENDOR, AltexSoft] By contrast, TAAP "only accepts travel agencies with qualifying credentials: IATA, ARC, CLIA, or True" and has "no requirements in terms of a minimum number of sales." — [AltexSoft on Medium](https://altexsoft.medium.com/expedia-partner-solutions-for-travel-agencies-expedia-taap-templates-and-eps-rapid-api-bde62ea21608)

**Money demanded / deposits**
- [FIRST-HAND, founder post, ~Oct 2021, EPS/TAAP era] A small-startup founder writes that after looking at Expedia EPS/TAAP, Expedia's follow-up was an automated email asking for roughly USD 1.5 million per year (LinkedIn version, "for dedicated customer service") / about USD 2 million (Medium version) to provide access. The two versions of his own post disagree on the figure; single unverified anecdote; not a Rapid-application rejection. — [LinkedIn: Why Expedia EPS Is Bad For Your Business?](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-expedia-eps-bad-your-business-taap-review-shehab-ahmed); [Medium version](https://medium.com/@shehabtech/why-expedia-eps-is-bad-for-your-business-expedia-taap-review-6c78e3b35f60)

### Inferences
- The only criteria that appear in more than one independent-of-each-other source are **projected booking volume / revenue potential** and **business model fit (must generate bookings)**. Both sources are vendors, but they are not copies of each other and they match the 2019 AltexSoft list, so this is the best-supported hypothesis for what the questionnaire screens on.
- "Existing solution" and "website traffic" (AltexSoft 2019) and "business track record" (dev.to) suggest that a live, demonstrable product and existing bookings matter; nothing official confirms it.
- PCI compliance is an official *launch* requirement tied to payment model (Property Collect / card handling), not a documented *application* screen. Choosing a model that avoids card handling should reduce compliance burden, but there is no evidence it affects acceptance.
- No source mentions a credit check, security deposit or prepayment as an admission criterion. The USD 1.5–2M figure in the 2021 anecdote is unexplained and should not be generalised.

### Gaps
- No official minimum booking volume, room nights, revenue, years in business or traffic threshold exists publicly; none of the vendor blogs gives a number either.
- The actual fields of the Rapid application form and the follow-up questionnaire could not be retrieved (host blocked in this session; no third party has published them).
- No evidence either way on credit checks, deposits or bank guarantees for Rapid B2B partners.
- Whether a national travel-agent licence (as opposed to IATA/ARC) is checked: no source.

## 2. What does "when an opening becomes available" mean in practice? Is onboarding capacity limited or prioritised by volume?

### Takeaway
No public source reproduces or explains this email wording, and Expedia has made no statement about capping or pausing core Rapid Lodging onboarding. The capacity/priority reading is supported only indirectly: vendor claims that Expedia prioritises higher-volume partners and assigns its own engineering/account staff to each integration, plus Expedia's documented use of waitlists and "select partner" gating on other Rapid products.

### Cited Findings
- [Search result, negative] A search for the exact phrases "when an opening becomes available" / "can't support your access" / "not in a position to on-board you" together with Expedia Rapid returned no page containing them. — (no source; see Gaps)
- [OFFICIAL, Oct 2026] Rapid Activities API "is launching in beta for select partners. General availability is planned for early 2027"; "The API is in early access for selected partners. A waitlist is open to explore eligibility and access." — [Expedia IR press release](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-B2B-Announces-Product-Milestones-to-Expand-Its-Full-Trip-Platform--/default.aspx); [Rapid Activities API landing page](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/make-every-trip-more-memorable-with-rapid-activities-api)
- [OFFICIAL, 2026] Rapid Car API is similarly gated, with full release expected in early 2027. — [Rapid Car API landing page](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/rapid-car-api-for-scalable-car-rental-bookings)
- [VENDOR, phptravels, 2026] Claims Expedia "has paused new Travel Redirect API applications because of request volume." This is a different API (redirect/affiliate, not Rapid) and is unconfirmed by Expedia. — [phptravels](https://phptravels.com/expedia-api-integration)
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub, 2026] Claims Expedia "engages its own engineering team for each integration", "prioritises higher-volume partners", and "smaller platforms are frequently turned away." — [ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2026] Each approved Rapid partner has a named "business development manager" who enables the production key, and launch requires a site review by Expedia's support team — i.e. each onboarding consumes Expedia staff time. — [Rapid "Get started"](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started); [Launch requirements overview](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/launch-requirements)
- [OFFICIAL/PRESS, 2026] Expedia B2B is growing fast without needing long-tail partners: B2B gross bookings +19% and B2B revenue +23% in Q3 2026 (reported 4 Nov 2026); Q2 2026 B2B gross bookings USD 10.7bn, +21%, "the 20th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth"; partner base expanded to 75,000. — [Expedia Q3 2026 earnings release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001324424/000132442426000059/earningsrelease-q32026.htm); [Q2 2026 call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/expedia-expe-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Q3 2026 call highlights](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9172771/expedia-group-inc-expe-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-margins-raised-guidance-and-aidriven-growth)
- [PRESS, 2026] Management expects B2B growth to decelerate in Q4 2026 as it laps "robust promotional activity from large partners" in the prior year — B2B commentary is framed around large partners. — [GuruFocus Q3 2026 highlights](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9172771/expedia-group-inc-expe-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-margins-raised-guidance-and-aidriven-growth)
- [PRESS, May 2026] Expedia's B2B head Alfonso Paredes was promoted to President, B2B & Chief Commercial Officer, taking over global supply; B2B and supply operations were combined. An organisational change of this kind in 2026 could plausibly affect onboarding bandwidth, but no source says so. — [Skift, 11 May 2026](https://skift.com/2026/05/11/expedia-partner-biz-leader-promotion-lead-supply-b2b-takeover/); [ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/expedia-group-combines-b2b-and-supply-operations-under-alfonso-paredes/)

### Inferences
- The wording "we can't support your access ... when an opening becomes available" reads as a **resource/priority deferral** rather than a compliance rejection: it does not cite a failed requirement and explicitly keeps the application on file. This fits the vendor claims that each integration consumes a business-development manager and technical review, and that Expedia ranks applicants by projected volume. This is an inference; no source confirms it.
- The same wording being sent as a no-reply template after a questionnaire suggests the questionnaire answers (most plausibly projected volume and current booking base) placed the applicant below the current cut-off, not that anything was "wrong" with the application.
- Skift/PhocusWire coverage found is about Expedia's B2B growth and AI tooling; none discusses throttling of small-partner onboarding. Absence of coverage is not evidence of absence.

### Gaps
- No public instance of this email text, no Expedia statement on onboarding quotas, and no report of anyone being contacted later "when an opening became available".
- Unknown whether "review over the next few months" is an active re-review or boilerplate.
- No trade-press article (Skift, PhocusWire) on Rapid onboarding capacity or small-partner pauses was found.

## 3. First-hand accounts: who was rejected, why, and who was approved after reapplying?

### Takeaway
There are essentially no Rapid-era (2019–2026) first-hand accounts in public forums; the three individual accounts found are from 2010, 2016 and 2021 and concern predecessor programmes (EAN affiliate API, EPS/TAAP). No account of a successful second application — and therefore no evidence of what applicants changed — was found.

### Cited Findings
- [FIRST-HAND, Hacker News, 2010, EAN era] In "Ask HN: Hotel API's", a developer reports being accepted into the affiliate sections of several hotel programmes including Expedia but turned away from the APIs, with rejections citing an "Incomplete Site". — [HN item 1704189](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1704189)
- [FIRST-HAND, Hacker News, 2016, EAN era] A hotel employee who built a small local rate-comparison app says they obtained Expedia API access by presenting as a travel agent, and that Expedia later blocked the access after an update, citing a lack of bookings. — [HN item 11137314](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11137314)
- [FIRST-HAND, LinkedIn/Medium, ~Oct 2021, EPS/TAAP era] Startup founder's complaint that Expedia's partner process is a poor fit for small companies; reports an automated email quoting about USD 1.5M/year (or ~USD 2M in the Medium copy). Mostly about a TAAP/supplier booking dispute, not an application review. — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-expedia-eps-bad-your-business-taap-review-shehab-ahmed)
- [FIRST-HAND-ish, Quora, c. 2016, EAN era] An answer states "Expedia has stopped allowing more CIDs to be registered" and that a revamped affiliate site was being tested but not open to everyone. — [Quora: How do I get CID for Expedia API](https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-get-CID-Client-ID-for-Expedia-API)
- [FIRST-HAND, Hacker News, 2023] A team building its own hotel booking engine describes sourcing inventory from GDS feeds, bed banks and Booking.com rather than Expedia; no stated Expedia rejection. — [HN item 38063710](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38063710)
- [VENDOR, 2026] The only current statements that small applicants are "frequently declined/turned away" or that applications "stall or go unanswered" come from ZentrumHub and StayAPI, both of which sell an alternative route. — [ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/); [StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)
- [Search result, negative] Site-restricted and keyword searches for Reddit, Indie Hackers, Stack Overflow and LinkedIn accounts of Rapid rejections or re-approvals returned nothing relevant. — (no source; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- The two EAN-era anecdotes point the same way as the vendor claims: Expedia has, for at least 15 years, screened on **a complete live product** (2010) and **actual booking production** (2016). The consistency across eras is suggestive, not proof of current policy.
- The lack of public accounts is itself informative: Rapid applicants are companies under commercial discussion/NDA rather than hobby developers, so outcomes are rarely posted. The realistic sources of first-hand intelligence are private (certified technology partners, integrators, or an Expedia business-development contact), not forums.

### Gaps
- No documented case of a rejected Rapid applicant being approved on a second attempt, hence no evidence on what change (higher projected volume, different business-model description, different entity, live product, references) works.
- Reddit could not be searched natively in this session; the search tool returned no Reddit threads. A manual search inside r/travelagents, r/startups and r/SaaS, and YouTube, was not possible and remains worth doing.
- No YouTube first-hand material was found.

## 4. How long must one wait before reapplying, and is a repeat application from the same company/email reviewed or auto-declined?

### Takeaway
No source — official, vendor or first-hand — states a cooling-off period or a re-review policy for Rapid. The only resubmission rule found belongs to a different programme (Expedia Group Affiliate Program) and should not be assumed to apply.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, different programme, article roughly three years old per search metadata] The Expedia Group **Affiliate Program** help centre has an article "Can I resubmit an application after being rejected?" which, as summarised, says that in certain cases "you can submit an application again", and that the resubmission must use a new email address rather than the one used originally. This is the creator/affiliate programme, not Rapid, and the conditions were not readable in full. — [Expedia Group Affiliates help centre](https://help.affiliates.expediagroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/15361858567191-Can-I-resubmit-an-application-after-being-rejected)
- [VENDOR, StayAPI, 2026] Rapid approval "takes weeks to months" and applications "tend to stall or go unanswered." — [StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub, 2026] Partner application stage estimated at 2–4 weeks; whole direct route "several months". — [ZentrumHub guide](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/expedia-rapid-hotel-api-integration/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2026] Existing/approved partners are told to go through their "account manager or Partner Connect representative" for additional Rapid APIs — the human channel, not the web form, is the documented route once a relationship exists. — [Rapid Typeahead guide](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/typeahead/typeahead)

### Inferences
- The rejection email itself states the application stays under review "over the next few months", which implies the record is retained; a duplicate form submission from the same company shortly afterwards is therefore unlikely to be treated as new information unless the underlying facts (live product, booking volumes, business model) have materially changed. This is reasoning from the email wording, not from a source.
- The Affiliate Program rule ("resubmit with a new email address") hints that some Expedia application systems de-duplicate on email, but extending this to Rapid is speculative.

### Gaps
- No stated waiting period, no evidence on whether same-company/same-email reapplications are re-reviewed or auto-declined, and no evidence on whether applying through a different legal entity helps or is flagged.
- No named escalation contact or appeal route for declined Rapid applicants was found publicly.

## 5. Which questionnaire answers are known or reported to trigger rejection?

### Takeaway
Nothing is *known*: no one has published the questionnaire or tied a rejection to a specific answer. Reported (vendor) and historical (anecdotal) signals point to low projected volume, no existing bookings/live product, and non-booking (data-only) use cases; official material additionally shows that onward redistribution and public display of closed-user rates are contractually sensitive.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, StayAPI, 2026] The application asks "what you're building, your business model, and your projected booking volume" and is reviewed "against revenue potential"; data-only, market-analysis and monitoring projects are outside Rapid's model. — [StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)
- [VENDOR, ZentrumHub, 2026] Vetting is on "booking volume, market, and coverage"; higher-volume partners are prioritised. — [ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/expedia-hotel-api/)
- [VENDOR, AltexSoft, c. 2019] Factors: revenue and growth potential, existing solution, website traffic, average number of bookings, IATA and other compliance. — [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/expedia-taap-rapid-api-partner-solutions/)
- [FIRST-HAND, 2010 and 2016, EAN era] Historical rejection/cut-off reasons actually cited by Expedia to individuals: "Incomplete Site" and lack of bookings. — [HN 1704189](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1704189); [HN 11137314](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11137314)
- [OFFICIAL, current] Requirement GR10: "Member-only deals are to be sold exclusively in a closed-user environment (for example, via login to a site, mobile app, or agent tool)." — [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs?locale=en_US)
- [OFFICIAL, current] Requirement GR3: where agents book on a traveller's behalf, "a link to the downstream agent agreement should be provided for agents to review and accept prior to making a booking." Package rates must be identified as package rates at room level and agents must accept package-rate rules (AP4, BP11). — [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs?locale=en_US)
- [OFFICIAL/PRESS, 2019 onward] Under the Marriott wholesale-rate arrangement run by Expedia Partner Solutions (effective 15 Oct 2019), certified partners may not, without Expedia Group's prior written consent, redistribute Marriott rooms through an API to a site the partner does not control; the stated purpose was to stop wholesale rates leaking into retail channels. — [Expedia partner page on Marriott wholesale rates](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/taap-marriott-wholesalers); [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/marriott-expedia-wholesale-rate-distribution); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Hotel-News/Handing-Expedia-its-wholesale-sales-gives-Marriott-retail-rate-control)
- [OFFICIAL, different Expedia API] The XAP API terms forbid sharing or redistributing "the API or Travel Content to any third party for any reason." Shows Expedia's general stance on onward distribution; not the Rapid contract. — [XAP API terms](https://developers.expediagroup.com/xap-apis/api/resources/terms-and-conditions)
- [PRESS, May 2026] On AI: Expedia B2B announced an AI toolkit to be "rolled out to select partners in the coming months" and a B2B MCP server that its B2B CPTO said "is going live very soon, in the coming months"; one summary of coverage says partners wanting to plug in "need a clear use case and the people and technology to connect agents safely." — [Skift, 21 May 2026](https://skift.com/2026/05/21/expedia-to-launch-agentic-ai-tools-for-b2b-partners/); [Travel Weekly UK](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/expedia-group-b2b-unveils-ai-planning-tools); [Hospitality ON](https://hospitality-on.com/en/distribution/expedia-group-opens-its-inventory-agentic-ai-partners-avoid-being-left-behind)
- [OFFICIAL, 2026] Metasearch/redirect use cases are served by a separate product family (Travel Redirect API / XAP) with its own keys and terms, not by Rapid. — [XAP API terms](https://developers.expediagroup.com/xap-apis/api/resources/terms-and-conditions); [integrations.sh summary of Expedia APIs](https://integrations.sh/expedia.com/)

### Inferences
- Most likely negative signals, in descending order of evidential support: (1) low projected bookings/room nights; (2) no current booking production or live product; (3) a use case that displays rates without Expedia-fulfilled bookings (data, comparison, metasearch-like); (4) onward distribution to third-party sites or sub-agents the applicant does not control, especially of package/member-only rates. Items 1–3 rest on vendor and old anecdotal evidence; item 4 rests on official rate-control rules, not on any reported rejection.
- A **closed, login-gated agent tool** is explicitly named in Expedia's own requirements as a legitimate environment for member-only rates, so describing the product as a closed user group for vetted agents aligns with Expedia's rules rather than against them. The sensitive point is whether those agents are the applicant's own users under a downstream agent agreement (allowed, per GR3) or independent resellers re-exposing rates on their own sites (requires consent, per the Marriott terms).
- AI-assistant display of rates is, as of 2026, something Expedia is productising for "select partners" with its own tooling; a small applicant proposing to expose Rapid rates inside a third-party AI assistant is plausibly treated as non-standard, but no rejection for this reason is documented.

### Gaps
- The questionnaire itself is not public; no source links any specific answer to a rejection.
- No documented case of rejection for AI-assistant or metasearch use, or for sub-distribution, in the Rapid programme.
- The full text of the B2B standalone launch requirements (which would show exactly what Expedia expects of agent-facing tools and sub-agents) could not be read in this session.

## 6. Does Expedia treat B2B tools for travel agents differently, and does it redirect such applicants to TAAP?

### Takeaway
Expedia officially recognises B2B/agent-tool integrations as a Rapid use case (dedicated B2B launch requirements, "agent tool" named in the rules), so B2B-for-agents is not disqualifying by design. No evidence was found of an explicit policy of redirecting declined Rapid applicants to TAAP, although Expedia's and third parties' positioning clearly presents TAAP as the default product for agencies without scale or development resources.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, current] Rapid has a dedicated "B2B standalone launch requirements" page; general requirements reference agents booking on behalf of travellers (GR3) and "agent tool" as a closed-user environment (GR10). — [B2B standalone launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/b2b-standalone); [Lodging launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/lodging-launch-reqs?locale=en_US)

### Inferences
- For an applicant that is itself a travel agency with a self-built agent tool, Expedia has two ready-made alternatives to a direct Rapid contract: (a) TAAP for the agency's own bookings, and (b) access via a certified technology partner. A deferral of the direct application is consistent with Expedia expecting small agencies to use one of those until they show volume — but no source states that this is Expedia's reasoning.
- If true, the practically evidenced path to a stronger second application is to **build demonstrable Expedia booking production first** (via TAAP and/or a certified technology partner) and reapply or seek a business-development introduction with real numbers. This follows from the volume/track-record criteria in Section 1; it is not attested by any applicant's account.
- The distinction Expedia's rules draw is not B2B vs B2C but **who controls the point of sale**: an agent tool the applicant operates for logged-in agents is a recognised model; a platform that pushes Rapid rates onward to other companies' sites needs explicit consent.

### Gaps
- No evidence of Expedia explicitly redirecting Rapid applicants to TAAP in rejection emails or calls.
- Not established whether TAAP production is visible to, or counted by, the Rapid partnerships team when reviewing a later application.
- Not established whether certified technology partners accept small agent-tool clients on workable commercial terms, or whether Expedia must separately approve each of their downstream clients.
- Official TAAP eligibility criteria for the applicant's country were not retrieved.
